## Figure 1

Figure 1 | Schematic overview of the cell membrane–camouflaged UCNP platform for intracellular thermometry. A cell membrane–camouflaged UCNP platform is employed for ratiometric temperature sensing within living cells. The preserved luminescence emission ratio (FIR, I₅₂₅/I₅₄₅) enables accurate temperature readout. This modular system allows selective targeting and temperature mapping at specific subcellular organelles, including mitochondria, the endoplasmic reticulum, and the Golgi apparatus, thereby facilitating precise intracellular thermometry.

## Figure 5

Figure 5 Organelle-resolved in situ calibration for ratiometric intracellular thermometry in BT474 cells. (A) Schematic illustration of the calibration approach. Using UCNP thermometry in FIR-based protocols, the ratiometric images of live cells are acquired as ln(I₅₂₅/I₅₄₅) for temperature-dependent signals. In situ fixation-based calibration in BT474 cells is performed at a controlled temperature (25–45 °C), and the developed organelle-specific calibration functions are used. (B) Representative raw dual-channel UCNP emission images, captured at predetermined temperatures for in situ calibration. The 525 nm emission channel in the top row; 545 nm in the bottom row. The imaging conditions were maintained constantly across all temperature points. (C) In situ organelle-resolved calibration curves for mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and Golgi apparatus of BT474 cells were generated. Independent calibration was carried out for each organelle (n = 10 cells per organelle), and ratiometric readings were taken at several temperatures and represented with ln(I₅₂₅/I₅₄₅) vs. inverse temperature (1000/T). Each solid-colored line shows the cell-specific calibration function applied to the follow-up temperature analysis; dashed grey lines show the median-slope reference trend for each organelle, indicating agreement between single-cell calibrations (reference trends are not used for temperature conversion).

## Figure 6

Figure 6 Organelle temperature signatures by breast-cancer subtypes and their metabolic interpretation. (A) Diagram illustrating metabolic phenotypes with different quantities of mitochondrial heat generated. From left to right: Ox Phos-active cells, intermediate metabolic activity, and Warburg-biased aerobic glycolysis. While increased mitochondrial respiration results in higher intracellular heat production, greater lactate production (Warburg effect) is associated with reduced mitochondrial heat output. (B) Quantification of organelle temperatures in breast cancer cell lines (BT474 (HER2+), MCF-7 (HER2−), and MDA-MB-231 (triple-negative breast cancer, TNBC)) representing different molecular subtypes. Temperatures of mitochondria (Mito), endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and Golgi apparatus were quantified using organelle-resolved in situ ratiometric thermometry. Each dot is for a single cell; black bars indicate mean ± SEM. The dashed gray line shows the reference temperature level of 37 °C (incubator temperature). Statistical significance was evaluated by paired t-tests with Holm correction (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ***P < 0.0001). Clear organelle-specific temperature hierarchies were noted across cell lines. MDA-MB-231 cells exhibited a general reduction in organelle temperatures, consistent with a Warburg-biased metabolic phenotype and diminished mitochondrial heat generation.
